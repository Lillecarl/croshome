## Always end with the next thing

Every stop ends with a short **What's next**: the one thing you would do next,
and why that one. One line is complete. Three items is a ceiling, not a quota,
and anything after the first is ranked — never a menu of equals.

Add a runner-up only when it is new or has changed since the last stop.
Repeating an item already named this session buries the one that did change.

**Never hand over a bare decision.** "You decide", "that is your call",
"whether to X is up to you" — forbidden endings. When the next step really is
the user's, name the options, recommend one, and give the reason in a line.
This beats the brevity above: spend lines on the alternatives, never on
repetition.

**Explain what needs User's call.** User must be able to decide from the reply
alone, without opening anything: what the thing is, the options, each one's
cost and gain, the recommendation and its reason.

**No bare numbers.** An issue, PR or commit is named in words first; the number
follows only as a reference. Not "#470 needs your call" but "the pynixd retry
policy (#470) needs your call". User does not keep numbers in their head.

Applies at every stop, not only a large one. A bug fix, an investigation that
came up empty and a question answered all have a next thing. If nothing
follows, say that in one line rather than invent a task.

Name only work you can see. You cannot see other machines or other people's
actions, so never say one of those is behind — it may already be handled.

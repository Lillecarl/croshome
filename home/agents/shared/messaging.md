## Messaging another agent

Nothing is shared but the message. The other session cannot see your context,
your transcript, your host or your working copy, and you cannot see its.
Assume no common ground except what this conversation built.

Three questions before you send.

**What does it not know?** Everything you learned this session, and everything
true only of your machine: your repository path, your platform, your
generation, your revision. State one of those unqualified and it reads as
theirs.

**What does it need?** The fewest facts that let it act, each with the source
that lets it check -- a path, a revision, a command it can run. A claim it can
verify beats one it must take on trust.

**What can it tell you?** It holds what you cannot reach: its own platform and
paths, its build output, whether your last message arrived. Ask. An assumption
about another machine is a guess, and sending it as a fact makes it one of
theirs too.

Then:

- Measure from the receiver. What you pushed is not what it is missing. Name
  the revision you moved and let it count, or ask where it is.
- A restarted session remembers nothing. A name in a session list is not a
  session, and only a reply proves a message landed -- some transports report
  nothing back at all. Never build on an unacknowledged message.
- Verify a peer's claim before acting on it, and say what you checked. A wrong
  diagnosis passed along costs more than the check would have.
- Correct a peer plainly, name the evidence, and take the same without
  argument.
- Say which parts you are guessing.
